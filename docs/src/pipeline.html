Each step reads the scene folder, stages its work in .gausser/tmp/, and replaces its output only after it succeeded. Run them one by one (gausser STEP) or let gausser run run every step that is missing or out of date.

gausser run

Runs frames (with --video), mask (with --prompt), solve, dense (with --dense), train, lod (--lod), mesh (--mesh, implies --dense) and map (--map), in that order. Every step's options are accepted.

  • A step is skipped when its output is up to date (rules on Scene and state). Once one step runs, every later step runs too.
  • --from STEP redoes that step and everything after it, e.g. gausser run --from solve --matcher sequential.
  • --import first moves photos lying in the scene folder into images/.
  • If COLMAP places fewer than half the images, solve raises and run stops before a wasted training run.
  • Without --prompt, no new masks are made but existing masks are still used; --no-masks ignores them.
Why one unattended command: an overnight run can fail at any step. Rerunning gausser run continues where it stopped, because finished outputs are files that are up to date.

gausser frames SOURCE

Picks the frames to train on. SOURCE is a video, a folder of videos, a folder of photos (copied whole), a QooCam 8K .MOV or photo folder, or an iPhone LiDAR scan folder. A folder's videos include those in its subfolders. Output: images/ plus frames.json (every pick with its time, motion and sharpness score).

How it chooses

  1. Analysis pass. ffmpeg decodes the video once to grey 1280 px frames. Each frame gets a focus score (variance of the Laplacian) and OpenCV DIS optical flow tracks a point grid.
  2. Motion ladder. Ten displacement thresholds are tracked at once. Each threshold cuts the video into windows where the camera has moved that far; the sharpest frame of each window is the pick.
  3. Level. --spacing low|medium|high picks a starting level; the level moves finer or coarser until the count lies between --min-frames (150) and --max-frames (600). Still stretches still give a frame every 3 s.
  4. Extraction pass. ffmpeg writes only the picked frames at full size, with the same input options so frame numbers match (a test checks this against pixel content).

Options: --range 1.5-32.5 (repeatable, seconds or m:ss) keeps parts of one video; --every N takes the sharpest of every N frames instead; --overlap 80 is for straight-down drone video: a frame whenever the next would share only 80% of the view, without frame-count limits. HDR (HLG/PQ) video is mapped to SDR.

360°, QooCam, LiDAR and drone sources add their own outputs (panos/, arkit/, GPS in EXIF): see Capture types. New frames delete everything built from the old ones.

Why own keyframe selection: a walk-around video at 30 fps has thousands of near-identical frames. Picking by camera motion gives enough overlap for COLMAP without tuning a frame rate per clip.

gausser mask PROMPT

SAM3 finds the prompted subject in every image and writes two mask sets: masks/<stem>.png for Brush (white = train) and colmap/masks/<name><ext>.png for COLMAP (the double extension is required).

gausser solve

COLMAP structure-from-motion: feature_extractor (one camera per source video in frames.json, else one shared camera; the image list, masks), a matcher, then the mapper. Separate cameras matter when several phones or lenses filmed one subject. The largest model becomes sparse/0; database.db records which images it used.

--matcherchosen by auto whennotes
spatialmost images have drone GPS (DJI photos, frames from a DJI .SRT)pairs images with their GPS neighbours; the global mapper (GLOMAP) is used. Phone GPS only georeferences: it is metres off and on one handheld set GLOMAP merged two passes 3 m apart

vocab_treeneverlarge unordered photo sets

Each camera gets its own COLMAP camera model: frames per source video, photos per phone model and EXIF focal length (a 3× lens is another camera). Photos of one lens taken at several sizes (48 MP and 12 MP) are first shrunk to the smallest, with their masks; the originals move to images-full/. As two cameras, the 12 MP group's focal came out 2× wrong on a one-sided set.

After mapping, solve moves the model when it knows better: 360° models are levelled (gravity +y), iPhone scans are snapped to ARKit (metres), and drone models are georeferenced into EPSG:3067 or UTM (sparse/geo.json). The mapper writes about 20 snapshots, which the monitor shows as the model grows. --no-masks solves on whole images and marks the model sparse/no-masks.

Why the half-registered stop: training on a fraction of the cameras gives a partial, smeared splat after an hour. More frames (--spacing high) or another matcher is the fix.

gausser dense

OpenMVS: colmap image_undistorter, InterfaceCOLMAP, then DensifyPointCloud (patch-match stereo on Metal) fuses a depth map per image into dense/scene_dense.ply. --resolution-level 1 (default) works on half-size images, about 4× faster than 0. Example: 350 iPhone 4K frames gave 32M points in 4 min. Depth maps are deleted after fusion.

Use it for scenes without masks (rooms, streets) and before mesh. train --init auto then starts Brush from 500,000 random dense points.

gausser lod [PLY]

splat-transform decimates the newest export to each --levels percentage (100,50,25) and bundles a streamed PlayCanvas LOD set into runs/lod/<time>/lod-meta.json, in chunks of --chunk-count thousand splats and --chunk-extent metres. Use it for web viewers that stream large splats.

gausser mesh

OpenMVS ReconstructMesh (Delaunay and a graph cut on what the cameras see through), RefineMesh (moves the surface until the photos agree; CPU only, slow, --no-refine skips it) and TextureMesh (a photo per triangle) into runs/mesh/<time>/mesh.glb. --faces 30000 decimates before texturing. Needs dense/. A crop cuts the surface before refining; --no-crop ignores it (for example to map more ground than the cropped splat shows).

gausser map [PLY|GLB]

An orthophoto and a surface model (DSM) of a georeferenced scene (drone GPS), from one of three sources (--source):

  • photos (the default when there is a mesh): each map cell takes its height from the mesh surface and its colour straight from the photo that sees it best: visible by a depth test, most straight down, near the image centre, and sharpest. The choice is made on a 5 cm grid with smoothed scores, and photos cross-fade over 0.4 m where it changes. --photos DIR samples full-resolution copies (for example the camera's 48 MP originals when images/ holds reduced ones). Cells no photo sees take the mesh texture or the splat. Default 2 cm cells.
  • mesh: the textured OpenMVS mesh rendered straight down, 2×2 samples per cell, as OpenDroneMap does. The highest surface wins, so roofs sit over their footprints (a true orthophoto).
  • splat (the default without a mesh): every Gaussian projects to a 2D Gaussian on the ground and is blended top first. Softer on large scenes, but needs no mesh.

The mesh and photo maps fill the mesh's holes (glass, deep shadow, thin structures) from the newest splat and cover the whole mesh; the photo map also bridges holes up to 15 m wide in the surface (the mesh often misses ground beside buildings) so the photos can still be laid on them. A whole flight at 2 cm takes about 7 minutes and 22 GB of memory (Set 2: 299 × 283 m, 94 photos at 48 MP); --plot limits them to the crop box, for example to map the plot at --resolution 0.01. run --map builds the best set in one go: the dense cloud, the splat (cropped when there is a crop), a mesh of the whole flight (mesh --no-crop) and the photo map. Each map run writes ortho.tif and dsm.tif (Cloud Optimized GeoTIFFs in the scene's CRS), Web Mercator previews (ortho-web.webp, and dsm-web.webp coloured by height and hill-shaded) for the monitor's Map tab, map.html (the orthophoto over satellite imagery or OpenStreetMap), map.json and README.txt, which says what made each file.
