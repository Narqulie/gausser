Every capture enters through gausser frames SOURCE (or photos dropped into images/). From there the pipeline is the same; what differs is how frames are cut, what extra data comes along, and how solve places the model.

Soft or hazy splat? Check the input before tuning training: raw frame resolution, capture mode, the frame extraction and the camera settings. A longer training run cannot add detail the frames do not have.

Phone video walk-around

gausser run --video IMG_8779.MOV --prompt statue --monitor
  • Walk slowly around the subject at two or three heights; keep it in frame and the shutter fast (good light).
  • 4K at 30 fps beats 1080p at 60 fps: frames picks by motion, so resolution matters more than frame rate.
  • Example: a 75 s iPhone walk-around gave 333 frames, and COLMAP registered all 333.
  • Photos (still, 4032 px) usually give sharper splats than video frames when you can stand still for each shot.

Folder of photos

gausser ~/photos/statue          # guided; offers to move loose photos into images/
gausser run --import --prompt statue

Photos are used whole: no selection. Overlap of about 60–80% between neighbours works; every photo needs a unique file stem (a.jpg and a.png collide in the masks). AppleDouble ._* files on exFAT drives are ignored.

360° equirectangular video

gausser frames pano.mp4                      # 2:1 frames → panos/ + 14 views each in images/
gausser mask person --exclude --dilation 8   # the operator moves with the camera
gausser solve
gausser train --exclude-masks

Stitched 2:1 frames are kept in panos/; images/ gets 14 pinhole views per frame (cNN_<frame>.jpg: 4 directions at −35°, 0° and 35° pitch plus straight up and down, 90° wide; a 5760 px frame gives 1440 px views). solve treats the 14 views of a frame as a rig with known rotations, so COLMAP solves one pose per frame, then levels the model so gravity is +y.

Why mask the operator: the operator stays in the same place in every frame and would match as a fixed object. mask --exclude therefore deletes the model in a 360° scene, and solve applies masks-exclude/ to the features too.

Kandao QooCam 8K raw video and photos

gausser frames Q360_20261105_….MOV --range 1.5-32.5 --range 0:57-1:20
gausser frames "/Volumes/QooCam 8K/PHOTO/Kitchen"
  • No stitching: frames reads the camera's own lens calibration (kfix LENS) from the file and puts the two 3840×3840 fisheye streams side by side.
  • The camera stores each lens image mirrored; frames flips it back on ingest. Without that the splats come out mirrored.
  • Each lens gives 7 views (its axis and a ring of 6 at 55°), real pinhole cameras at the lens centre; solve knows the 2.95 cm between the lenses, so the model is in metres.
  • frames writes masks-exclude/: black outside the lens circle and in a 35° cone around the grip. train uses it even without --exclude-masks.
  • For several passes, list them with their times and orientation first, then cut them with --range.
  • Photos (3840×7680, lens 0 above lens 1): stand still for each shot, about 20 cm apart.

iPhone LiDAR scans (Stray Scanner)

gausser frames ~/Downloads/a9aca2e1bd   # rgb.mp4 + odometry.csv + depth/ + confidence/
gausser solve                           # snapped to ARKit: metres, gravity +y
gausser train                           # Brush starts from the LiDAR points

The Camera app keeps no depth; record with Stray Scanner and AirDrop the folder. frames picks frames from rgb.mp4 (1920×1440) and writes arkit/: each frame's ARKit pose, its high-confidence depth map and points.ply (one LiDAR point per 1 cm voxel). solve starts from ARKit's focal length, then snaps the model onto ARKit's cameras: rotation from the mean per-camera rotation, scale from the median LiDAR/COLMAP depth ratio. It prints how far the two disagree; above 10 cm it warns.

DJI drone mapping

gausser frames ~/Desktop/DJI_0003.MP4 --overlap 80   # or drone photos in images/
gausser run --map
open runs/map/*/map.html
  • GPS. Turn on Video Subtitles in DJI Fly. The drone writes DJI_xxxx.SRT next to each video, one position per frame; frames interpolates it into each frame's EXIF.
  • Georeference. solve fits a similarity from the cameras onto GPS in EPSG:3067 (Finland) or UTM, dropping outliers, and records the CRS and origin in sparse/geo.json. Axes: x east, y down, z north, in metres. When that fit tilts the cameras' up more than 10° off gravity (phone GPS along a walk cannot fix the roll about it), it keeps the cameras' up and takes only heading, scale and position from GPS; geo.json records "up": "cameras" and the tilt.
  • Flying. Parallel lines, 70% side overlap, steady height and speed, camera at −90°, shutter 1/500 s or faster. A crosshatch or −45° pass keeps the model from bending into a bowl. Snow, water and shadows give COLMAP little to match.
  • DJI Mini 4 Pro + RC 2 runs no live mapping apps; only DJI Fly KMZ waypoint missions (≤ 200 waypoints, one battery). In YMapper use Overlap 85 / Sidelap 60 to get a real ~80/70.

Leaving people out

gausser mask person --exclude --dilation 8
gausser train --exclude-masks

masks-exclude/<stem>.png is black over every instance of the prompt grown by --dilation px, and white elsewhere and wherever the subject mask in masks/ is white (SAM3 may call a bronze statue a "person"). 8 px covers smeared edges and shadows. It leaves masks/ and the model alone, except in 360° scenes (above).

A masked run on an unmasked solve (mask --miss drop, solve --no-masks, train) starts from the whole scene's COLMAP points and keeps opaque background splats that the masked loss never removes; crop the result with gausser crop, or train the outside of the masks transparent with gausser train -- --alpha-mode transparent. train leaves images with an all-black mask out, since in that mode they would teach the subject to vanish.
